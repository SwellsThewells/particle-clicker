The shape of the Higgs field

The Higgs boson interacts with itself. How strongly it does decides the shape of the Higgs potential, the "Mexican hat" that gives the other particles their masses, and maybe the fate of the universe.

To measure it, experiments look for collisions that make two Higgs bosons at once. That happens more than a thousand times less often than making just one.

A job for the High-Luminosity LHC

ATLAS and CMS have not seen Higgs pairs yet, but they have narrowed down how strong the self-coupling can be. With the High-Luminosity LHC they expect to see them, and to measure the self-coupling to within about 30%.

A future collider built to make millions of Higgs bosons could do even better.
