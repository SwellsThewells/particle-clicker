Particles made of glue

Gluons carry the strong force that holds quarks together. Photons carry the electromagnetic force but have no electric charge themselves, so they ignore each other. Gluons are different: they carry the charge of the strong force, called colour, so they attract each other.

Quantum chromodynamics, the theory of the strong force, therefore predicts particles made of gluons alone, without any quarks: glueballs.

Hard to spot

A glueball would look a lot like an ordinary meson made of a quark and an antiquark, and the two can even mix. Calculations with lattice QCD predict that the lightest glueball weighs a little less than two protons.

There are several candidates, such as the f0(1500) and the f0(1710). In 2024 the BESIII experiment in Beijing measured the properties of a particle called X(2370) and found that they match the predictions for a glueball.
