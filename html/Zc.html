You discovered tetraquarks!

Four quarks

Ordinary hadrons are made either of three quarks, like the proton, or of a quark and an antiquark, like the J/ψ. The quark model has allowed more exotic combinations since the 1960s, but they were very hard to find.

In 2003 the Belle experiment in Japan found the X(3872), a particle that did not fit in as a simple pair of a charm quark and an anticharm quark. Later came charged particles such as the Z(4430), confirmed by LHCb in 2014, and the Zc(3900). They contain a charm quark and an anticharm quark but also carry electric charge, so they need at least four quarks.

Molecules or compact particles?

Physicists still debate how tetraquarks are built: as four tightly bound quarks, or as two mesons loosely stuck together, like atoms in a molecule.

In 2021 LHCb observed the Tcc+, a tetraquark with two charm quarks and two light antiquarks.
