You caught neutrinos made in a collider!

Neutrinos from the LHC

Collisions at the LHC make huge numbers of neutrinos, and many fly off straight along the line of the beams. But neutrinos hardly ever interact with anything, so nobody had ever caught one that came from a collider.

FASER is a small detector in an old, unused tunnel, 480 metres from the collisions in ATLAS, right on that line. Almost all other particles are stopped by the rock in between; the neutrinos pass straight through. In 2023 FASER reported the first direct observation of collider neutrinos: 153 of them, with a significance of 16 standard deviations.

The most energetic ever made

These are the most energetic neutrinos ever made by people, with energies from a few hundred GeV to a few TeV. Nobody had measured how neutrinos interact at these energies before.

Knowing that helps physicists understand the neutrinos that arrive from space, and how particles are made close to the beam, where the big detectors cannot see.
