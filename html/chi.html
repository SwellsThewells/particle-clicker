The missing mass

Galaxies spin faster than the matter we can see could hold together, and light bends around clusters of galaxies as if they were far heavier than they look. Most physicists think the missing mass is dark matter, about five times as much as ordinary matter.

Dark matter does not shine, absorb or reflect light. So far it has only been seen through its gravity.

Three ways to catch it

A collider could make dark matter particles, which would fly out unseen and leave missing energy behind. A detector deep underground could catch one bumping into an atom. And telescopes could spot what is left when two of them meet in space.

Experiments are trying all three. Supersymmetry, axions and whole hidden sectors of new particles are among the ideas they test.
