More directions

We know three directions of space: left and right, forwards and backwards, up and down. In the 1920s Theodor Kaluza and Oskar Klein showed that a fourth direction of space, curled up into a circle too small to notice, could bring gravity and electromagnetism together in one theory.

A garden hose looks like a line from far away, but an ant on it can also walk around it. Tiny extra dimensions could hide in the same way. String theory needs six of them.

Looking at the LHC

Gravity is far weaker than the other forces. One idea is that it leaks into extra dimensions that are much larger than the others. Then gravity would become strong at very short distances, and the LHC could make gravitons that escape into the extra dimensions, leaving missing energy behind, or even microscopic black holes that would evaporate at once.

Every particle would also get a tower of heavier copies, called Kaluza–Klein states. So far, the LHC has seen no sign of any of them.
