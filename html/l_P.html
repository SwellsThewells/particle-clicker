Two theories that don't fit

Quantum physics describes particles and the forces between them. General relativity describes gravity, space and time. Both pass every test, but they do not fit together.

Inside black holes and at the Big Bang, both are needed at once, and nobody knows for sure what happens there.

The Planck length

Quantum gravity should matter at the Planck length, about 1.6 × 10-35 metres: a hundred billion billion times smaller than a proton. String theory and loop quantum gravity are two of the ideas for how it works.

Testing them directly would take a collider far bigger than any that could be built, so physicists look for clues in black holes and in the light left over from the early universe.
