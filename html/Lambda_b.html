You saw matter and antimatter baryons behave differently!

Not quite mirror images

CP violation means that matter and antimatter do not behave exactly the same. It was first seen in 1964 in kaons, and later in B and D mesons: particles made of a quark and an antiquark. Baryons, made of three quarks like protons and neutrons, held out until 2025.

LHCb studied the beauty baryon Λb0 decaying into a proton, a kaon and two pions, and its antimatter partner decaying the same way. The two decay at slightly different rates: about 2.5% apart, with a significance of 5.2 standard deviations.

Why is there anything at all?

The Big Bang should have made just as much antimatter as matter, and the two would have destroyed each other. Something tipped the balance, and CP violation is one of the things that is needed for that.

The CP violation in the Standard Model is far too small to explain the matter around us. Every new place where it shows up is a chance to find out where the rest comes from.
