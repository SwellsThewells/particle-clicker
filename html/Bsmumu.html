You saw one of the rarest decays ever measured!

Three in a billion

The Bs0 meson is made of a beauty antiquark and a strange quark. It can turn into a muon and an antimuon, but in the Standard Model this only happens through rare quantum loops: about three times in every billion decays.

Experiments searched for this decay for decades. CMS and LHCb each came close in 2013, and in 2015 they combined their data to observe it with a significance of 6.2 standard deviations, in a paper published in Nature.

A window on new physics

New heavy particles, such as those predicted by supersymmetry, could join in the quantum loops and make the decay far more common. The measured rate agrees with the Standard Model, which ruled out many of these ideas.

Its cousin, the B0 meson, should decay into two muons about thirty times less often still. That is the next challenge.
