Melting protons

Quarks and gluons are normally locked inside protons and neutrons. Heat nuclear matter to about two trillion degrees, more than a hundred thousand times hotter than the centre of the Sun, and they break free into a hot, dense soup: the quark–gluon plasma.

The whole universe was filled with this plasma for the first few millionths of a second after the Big Bang, before it cooled down enough for protons and neutrons to form.

How to make one

To recreate these conditions, physicists collide heavy nuclei such as lead or gold at very high energies. In 2000 CERN presented evidence for a new state of matter from its heavy-ion experiments at the SPS. At the RHIC collider in the United States, experiments then found that the plasma behaves like an almost perfect liquid rather than a gas.

Since 2010 the ALICE experiment at the LHC studies it at even higher temperatures, using collisions of lead nuclei.
