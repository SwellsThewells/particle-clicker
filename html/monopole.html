A magnet with one pole

Cut a bar magnet in half and you get two smaller magnets, each with a north and a south pole. A magnetic monopole would be a particle with only one of them: a magnetic charge, just as the electron has an electric charge.

In 1931 Paul Dirac showed that if even a single monopole exists anywhere in the universe, electric charge must come in whole steps, which it does. Many theories that unify the forces predict monopoles, but nobody has found one yet.

The hunt at the LHC

The MoEDAL experiment sits next to LHCb, around one of the LHC's collision points. It uses stacks of plastic sheets that a monopole would punch a trail of tiny holes through, and blocks of aluminium in which a monopole could get stuck. The blocks are later passed through a very sensitive magnetometer.

In 2022 MoEDAL searched for monopoles made in the enormous magnetic fields of lead-ion collisions. It found none, which tells physicists how heavy they would have to be.
