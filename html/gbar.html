You found out which way antimatter falls!

Up or down?

Every particle has an antiparticle with the same mass and the opposite charge. Einstein's theory of gravity says that antimatter should fall exactly like ordinary matter, but for a long time nobody had checked. Some physicists wondered whether antimatter might even fall upwards.

The experiment is much harder than it sounds. Gravity is extremely weak compared with electric and magnetic forces, so you need neutral atoms of antimatter, very cold and held in a trap without touching anything made of matter.

ALPHA-g

At CERN's Antiproton Decelerator, the ALPHA collaboration built ALPHA-g: a tall, vertical magnetic trap for antihydrogen atoms. They slowly weakened the magnets at the top and bottom and counted where the atoms escaped. In 2023 they reported that more atoms left through the bottom, just as expected if antimatter falls down like everything else.

The result rules out antigravity, but it is not yet very precise. ALPHA-g and two other CERN experiments, AEgIS and GBAR, are working on much more accurate measurements.
